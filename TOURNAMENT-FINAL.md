# The 104-Product Tournament — Playbook-Gated

**Method:** the unified funnel from `PLAYBOOK.md`, applied in the operators' order — angles first, then trend, then saturation, then the CVS test, then margin.

---

## Round 0 — The 6-angle pre-gate (104 → 14)

Per Saamir, angle count is a *product* criterion, not a marketing one: a good product yields ~1 winning creative in 10, a weak one 1 in 100. So nothing reached the tournament without six genuinely distinct angles — distinct *motivations or audiences*, not rephrasings. "Look younger" and "reduce wrinkles" count as one.

Kill flags applied first (Biaheza / Saamir / Welch): `retail` (on a CVS/Target shelf today), `size` (size variance → returns + ad friction), `old` (wow factor decayed), `policy` (regulated claim or restricted category).

**Result: 63 killed on flags, 27 failed the angle gate, 14 advanced. An 87% cull.**

The angle gate is where most "obvious" dropshipping products die. Cable organiser: one angle. Herb stripper: one. Garlic chopper: two. Magnetic gym phone mount: three. These are the products beginners pick, and they cannot support a creative testing programme.

Notable near-misses at 5 angles: smart sleep ring, derma stamp, beard roller, hip thrust belt, portable drag sled, cold plunge, TMJ device, posture wearable, pocket printer.

## 🏆 Winner — Sequential pneumatic compression recovery boots (athlete segment)

Full-leg, multi-chamber, sequential inflation. Entering between the $85 massagers and the $279–899 recovery systems.

### Against every playbook gate

| Gate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Old, painful problem | ✅ Sore, heavy, swollen legs — ancient and universal |
| Solution new to the customer | ✅ Pneumatic compression was a $900+ clinic and pro-team device; the consumer tier is recent |
| Already selling with data | ✅ Strongly — the whole category is transacting |
| **CVS test** | ✅ **Passes decisively.** You cannot buy recovery boots at Target or a pharmacy |
| Six+ angles | ✅ recovery · circulation · swelling/oedema · endurance athletes · travel and standing-job legs · varicose appearance |
| Saturation | ✅ Athlete segment leader at ~1,000 reviews; Normatec at 592 |
| ≥2.5x margin | ✅ borderline — see below |
| Price-reference position | ✅ You are the *cheap* side of $279–899, not the expensive side of $85 |
| Wow factor / demo | ✅ Sequential inflation is visually and audibly satisfying. Legs only — no face, faceless-UGC compatible |
| Upsell path | ✅ hip attachment, arm sleeves, carry case, extended warranty → raises a $199 order toward $280 |
| Efficacy | ✅ **Real.** Pneumatic compression genuinely aids recovery. No overclaiming required — this is the key difference from the sauna blanket |

### Numbers

- **Entry price: $179–229.** Below FIT KING at $279, far below Normatec at $899, and clearly above the $85 massagers so you are not competing with QUINEAR's review moat.
- **Source estimate: $50–90** for a four-chamber full-leg unit. At $199 retail that is roughly 2.5x on landed cost — exactly Hampton's floor, no more.
- **Upsells** are what make the economics comfortable rather than tight.

### The three real risks

1. **Margin is at the floor, not above it.** 2.5x is the minimum, and freight erodes it. This needs a real RFQ before committing — if landed cost comes in above $90, the thesis weakens materially.
2. **Size variance exists.** FIT KING sells S/M/L. This is a Biaheza kill flag and must be engineered out in sourcing — specify one-size with zip and velcro adjustment across a wide calf/thigh range, or accept the return rate.
3. **Freight is the weak point.** 3–5kg and bulky. This is the single biggest threat to the margin, and the reason the estimate above is a range rather than a number.

### What must not be claimed

Do not market for **lymphedema, DVT, or blood clots** — those are medical claims that put the product into regulated-device territory. Stay on recovery, soreness, heavy legs, and post-workout comfort. Same discipline as the hair cap's 510(k) line.
