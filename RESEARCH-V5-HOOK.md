A product with a visible mechanism accumulates high-view shorts; one whose benefit is invisible or slow does not, regardless of search demand.

## 2. The hair cap's regulatory position — the barrier *is* the moat

This is what kept it in second place in round 4, so it needed resolving properly.

- Light therapy devices for **hair growth are Class II** and require **510(k) clearance**. General-wellness light products can be Class I, but hair-growth claims do not qualify for the general wellness exemption.
- Claims must **match the specific clearance** of the device — general red light therapy language does not transfer.
- The **FTC is actively enforcing**: warning letters to Spectra Therapy LLC (July 2025) and Dr. Joel Kaplan (January 2024) specifically flag hair growth among systematically overclaimed categories.

Three routes:

| Route | Cost / time | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Own 510(k) | ~$30–100k+, months | Too slow for a first product |
| **White-label an OEM that already holds clearance** | sourcing diligence only | **The practical path** |
| Sell with no hair claims | free | Kills the hook — pointless |

Route 2 is the recommendation: require documented 510(k) in the RFQ and verify the K-number in FDA's database before ordering. Suppliers who cannot produce one are disqualified.

**This is why the shelf is open.** Median 49 reviews in a category where the incumbent sells at $499 with 3,486 reviews is not an oversight — it is the regulatory barrier keeping generic sellers out. Clearing it properly is a durable advantage, which is exactly what "own a brand" requires. Compare the beauty pillow, where nothing stops the next entrant.

### Margin

At $99 it nets 13.5% — below target. At **$149 it nets 24.1%**, inside the band, and $149 sits far under iRestore at $499–1,998 and Theradome at $529–995, so you are the cheap side of a published reference price.

## 4. Track 2 — five hooks to test the pillow before spending

The pillow is not dead. It has the best margin and no barriers; it only failed the hook gate. Since the gate is a proxy, test it directly. **Post these against a placeholder before ordering inventory.** If any clears ~50k organic views, the proxy was wrong and the pillow's margin advantage makes it the better business.

Each is shot-first, silent-readable, ≤ 7 seconds to the hook.

### Hook 1 — "The 8-hour crease" *(strongest, needs a face)*
> **Text on screen:** "You spend 8 hours a night pressing your face into a pillow."
- 0–2s: overhead, face pressed hard into an ordinary pillow, skin visibly bunched
- 2–4s: sit up, close-up on the crease lines across the cheek
- 4–7s: cut to the contoured pillow — face suspended, cheek untouched
- **Test:** does the crease close-up stop the scroll?

### Hook 2 — "Silk pillowcases fix the wrong problem" *(faceless-capable)*
> **Text:** "Your silk pillowcase fixes friction. It doesn't fix pressure."
- 0–3s: hand drags across silk — smooth, no grab
- 3–5s: same hand *presses down* into silk — fabric and skin compress anyway
- 5–8s: press into the contour — hand passes into the void, no compression
- **Why it may work:** attacks a product 484 listings and 2,743 median reviews deep. Borrowed attention.

### Hook 3 — "What is that pillow?" *(pure object, fully faceless)*
> **Text:** "Why does this pillow have a hole in it?"
- 0–2s: slow rotate on the odd silhouette, clean background
- 2–5s: a tomato or soft fruit set on a flat pillow, weight applied — it deforms
- 5–8s: same fruit in the contour — weight applied, no deformation
- **Why:** substitutes an object for a face. Fully AI/faceless compatible.

### Hook 4 — "Sleep position audit" *(educational, high save rate)*
> **Text:** "Rank these sleep positions by how fast they age your face."
- Three quick cuts: stomach → side → back, each with a crease-severity caption
- Land on: "Back is best. Nobody stays on their back. So change the pillow instead."
- **Why:** ranking formats drive comments and saves, which is reach.

### Hook 5 — "The $289 pillow" *(price anchor)*
> **Text:** "There's a $289 pillow whose only job is to stop face wrinkles."
- 0–3s: the incumbent's price on screen
- 3–6s: "Here's what it actually is" — cross-section of contour foam
- 6–9s: your price
- **Why:** SLEEP & GLOW at $288.99 with 562 reviews is a real, checkable anchor. Comparison converts *for* you.

**Read the test as:** any hook >50k views = proxy was wrong, build the pillow. All under 10k = proxy was right, drop it. In between = the category needs paid support, so the margin advantage is spent on ads and the hair cap wins anyway.
