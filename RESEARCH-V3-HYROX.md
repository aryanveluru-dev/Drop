## 2. Demand gate — the injury thesis passes hard

- **43% of active HYROX athletes reported sustaining an injury** during training or competition (2025 peer-reviewed study, *Applied Sciences*).
- Clinics converge on the same primary injury: *"One of the most common injuries we see in HYROX athletes is medial tibial stress syndrome — better known as shin splints. They're incredibly common in HYROX athletes because of the running volume combined with high-impact exercises."*
- Mechanism is understood and specific: *"Rapid increases in running volume... a classic beginner's mistake."* HYROX is **70% first-timers**, largely strength-background athletes ramping running volume fast.
- Mechanistic detail: *"As your heel strikes, the tibialis anterior lowers your forefoot under control, absorbing impact, and when it fatigues, that load goes straight to your shin bone."*
- There is already a content narrative with market fit: *"The HYROX Injury Wave: What Social Media Isn't Showing You."*

At 2.5M projected athletes in 2026, a 43% injury rate implies roughly a million injured athletes a year. This is the strongest demand signal found in any round.

## 4. The structural finding

Across ~45 keywords over three rounds, the pattern is consistent:

**HYROX athletes' needs map almost entirely onto product categories that are decades old** — running (compression, socks, shoes), strength (belts, wraps, sleeves), recovery (massage, foam rolling, cupping). Those categories are owned by incumbents with five-figure review counts and floor pricing.

The categories that *are* open are open for bad reasons:
- **Sleds** — open because heavy and awkward to ship, i.e. thin demand (Round 2's failure)
- **Tib bars** — open because new, but already commoditising into $27 clones

**What HYROX genuinely creates that has no old category:** the race as a travel event (100+ events, 30+ countries, athletes flying internationally), the specific station combination, and the identity/community layer. Travel was named by *both* elite athletes as their number-one limiter — but the component products (compression socks, packing cubes) are themselves closed categories.

## 5. Best remaining candidate — and its honest ceiling

**A HYROX-specific lower-leg prehab kit**, sold as a system rather than a SKU: tibialis trainer + ankle/calf loading tool + a printed protocol keyed to running-volume ramp, at **$59–79**.

Why this configuration and not a bare tib bar:

- **Demand gate:** passes on the strongest evidence found — 43% injury rate, shin splints named as *the* HYROX injury, mechanism understood, 70% first-timers ramping volume.
- **Saturation gate:** the only non-closed category in the recovery sweep.
- **Price-reference gate:** a **bundle has no comparable SKU**. This is the specific defence against the $27 clone war — you are not listed against fourteen identical bars, and the buyer cannot price-check the combination.
- **Faceless UGC:** shins and feet only. Mechanical, no face, no body, no health claim.
- **Ties to the source material:** it productises Lauren Weeks' *"hour of PT work every day... unexciting... time consuming"* — the kit's real promise is compressing that hour, which is the actual articulated pain.

**Honest ceiling:**
1. The core component is a commodity steel bar. The bundle and the protocol are the differentiation, and both are copyable in a season.
2. It sells prevention, which converts worse than cure. People buy after their first injury, not before — so the buying trigger is post-injury, and the messaging has to meet them there.
3. Steel ships heavier than fabric. Cheaper than a sled, not as cheap as a sleeve.
4. Protocol content must avoid medical claims. Frame as training, not treatment.

## 6. What I would test before committing

The demand is proven; the product form is not. Before any inventory:
- TikTok Shop manual check on tib bar / prehab kit presence (`amzsat.py` measures Amazon only)
- Whether the buying trigger is genuinely post-injury — if so, "fix my shin splints" beats "prevent them," and that changes both the product and the creative
- Alibaba RFQ on a tib trainer + ankle strap combo to establish landed cost against a $59–79 target
